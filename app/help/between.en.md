# Between the fights

Everything that happens when nobody is rolling initiative: the session itself, the road,
the days off, the bastion, and the people the characters know. Most of it starts on the
party view (*Table*), in the world the table plays in.

---

## 1. The session

The **Session** button on the party view opens the session page.

- **Before play** it lists what to settle: last time's note, the date, timers still
  burning, bastion turns that are due, lifestyle a character could not pay, contacts
  nobody has named yet, downtime days still to spend, and anything left open (a chase,
  the shop, calls for checks, open quests).
- **Start the session** when the table is ready. The page then shows what has happened
  since: time passed, miles travelled, chases, traps, quests finished, contacts made.
- **End the session** writes today's session note: the recap of the fights and an
  *In the world* section from the log. It opens for you to add your own words.
- **Send to the players** puts the note, as it reads, into every character's journal,
  ready as next week's "previously on".

## 2. The clock and the calendar

The clock panel on the party view keeps the world's day and time. Move it on, rest the
party, start your own reminders; torches, lanterns and spells with a duration run out
against it. **Calendar** dates the days by a setting calendar your books print, with
holidays and seasons; a leap day the calendar marks comes every fourth year.

A rest variant in **World settings** changes how long a party rest takes: epic heroism
(5 minutes, 1 hour) or gritty realism (8 hours, 7 days, and a night on the road is only a
short rest). The other variant rules for characters sit there too: hero points, Honor and
Sanity, slow natural healing, healer's kit dependency and defeated, not dead.

## 3. On the road

**Set out** on the clock panel: how long, how fast, through which encounter table, how
often to check, with rations and water, mounted or aboard a ship.

- Each evening everyone eats from their pack. Hunger and thirst cost exhaustion by the
  world's rules, and that exhaustion stays until the character has eaten and drunk.
- An encounter check that comes up stops the party there; one press makes it an
  encounter to run.
- **Climate**: through extreme cold or heat, everyone makes a Constitution save at the
  end of every travelling hour, a failure costing a level of exhaustion; resistance (or
  cold weather gear against cold) spares them. **High altitude** halves the day's
  distance. The journey links the books' hazards it met.
- **Terrain** (from the encounter table, or chosen) brings the world's guide's rules for
  it. A **navigator** checks Wisdom (Survival) each morning; a failure sends the party
  astray, and the journey says how far. Characters ticked to **forage** look for food
  and water at each day's end: what they find replaces rations and tops up the water.
  Under the 2024 rules the terrain also caps the pace (mountains and swamps: slow).
- When an encounter comes up, the journey rolls its **distance** and works out
  **surprise**: who would be caught if the creatures lie in wait, and which creatures a
  party **moving stealthily** would catch unaware. Use it or not; it goes into the
  encounter's notes.
- The travel form chooses *mounted* by itself when every character has a mount.

**In a dungeon**, **Dungeon** on the clock panel sets the encounter checks while the party
explores: an encounter table (or none, and the creatures are your call), a check every 10
minutes, hour or more, and the d20 it comes up on (18 or more, as the guides suggest).
Every move of the clock then rolls the checks it passes — a step, a rest, a fight's
rounds, downtime — and **Check now** rolls one at once when the party makes a noise. A
check that comes up rolls the table for the party's level; one press makes it an
encounter, and no further checks come until you press **Dealt with**. Only you see the
checks, in the panel and the log. The ✕ leaves the dungeon.

## 4. Days off

- **Grant downtime** on the clock panel. Each character spends the days on its
  *Downtime* page: crafting with the right tools, training, scribing scrolls, research,
  recuperating, work, or the book's activities from carousing to crime.
- **Magic items** are crafted from a formula: *Give the formula* on a magic item's page
  hands it to the characters you tick, and their *Downtime* page then offers to craft it
  by your guide — its days and cost, and what the character still lacks (Arcana, the
  tool, the item's spells, a level).
- **Lifestyle** is chosen on the sheet's *Gear* tab and paid each day the clock passes
  off the road; what the purse lacks is logged as owed.
- **A shop** opens from the party view, at a share of the list price, with mundane gear
  of the world's books and magic items you put on the shelf.

## 5. The bastion

From level 5 a character builds the special facilities the 2024 rules allow on its
*Bastion* page and gives each an order; you run the turn. On *Maintain* the turn rolls a
bastion event and carries it out: coins, a guest, a shut facility, treasure. A bribe, an
opportunity, a request for aid or a magical discovery waits on the bastion page for the
player's decision.

## 6. People: contacts, factions, quests and followers

- **Contacts**: carousing's allies and enemies, a temple's favours and the rivals behind
  a starred complication land on the character's downtime page, to name, note and call
  on. Anyone else can be written down there too.
- **Factions** (from the session page): make one by hand, from a book's renown table
  (its rewards) or from the guide's example factions (their titles). Raise or lower
  each character's renown; the character's downtime page shows its title and the
  rewards it has reached.
- **Quests** (from the session page): who gave it, its steps, who is on it and its
  reward. Players read the quests you show them in their journal. **Done** hands out
  the XP, gold, items and renown, once. Claude can write quests into its drafts; they
  stay hidden until you show them.
- **Followers** (a button on the sheet): hirelings paid by the day, and companions,
  mounts and sidekicks from your books with their own hit points. A sidekick may take
  one of Tasha's sidekick classes; *Level up* raises its hit points, and *Into the fight*
  adds it to the character's running fight on the party's side.

## 7. Chases and traps

- **A chase** starts from the party view: the world's characters and creatures of your
  books as quarry and pursuers. A player takes their own turn from the sheet; the
  complication die and the escape checks come at the end of each turn and round.
- **A trap** in a chapter or on its page has **Spring it**: tick who is caught. It
  rolls the saves on their sheets, or lets the players roll them (*Players roll the
  saves*), and deals its damage with their resistances counted.
- **A disease or a poison** in a chapter or on its page has **Afflict**: tick who meets
  it, and roll their saves or let them catch it outright. A poison deals its damage and
  puts its conditions on for as long as its text says. A disease keeps quiet until its
  symptoms show on the clock, then brings its exhaustion and conditions, and each long
  rest or dawn rolls the save its text calls for — three successes, a level of exhaustion
  more or less, or a falling DC, as the books say. **Ends** on the sheet cures it. A
  **curse** is your own: *Put a curse on* in the sheet's Track view, with a name and what
  it does.
- **Madness** on the party view meets something horrifying: tick who faces it, set the
  saving throw and its DC, and add psychic damage if it is mental stress. Whoever fails
  takes the damage and the madness you chose — short-term (1d10 minutes), long-term
  (1d10 × 10 hours) or indefinite — each rolled on its own on your guide's table, its
  conditions put on. It shows in the sheet's Track view, wears off by the world's clock,
  and **Ends** there cures it.
- **Supernatural gifts** — a blessing, a charm, a boon — are given from the gift's page:
  *Grant*, with the uses and days its text names filled in. The sheet's Track view lists
  them; a charm's uses are spent there, and it vanishes with the last or when its time is
  up. *Take back* ends one.

## 8. Levelling

XP comes from fights and quests, and the sheet shows how far each character has to go.
For anything else — a riddle solved, a deal struck — **Award XP** on the party view gives
the characters ticked the same amount each, or shares a total out; untick whoever was absent.
A world set to **Level by milestones** awards and shows no XP; characters level when you
grant it.
